Where Is the Residence Card Used in Japan?

June 6, 2026

If you are a foreign national staying in Japan for an extended period, there is one document you will always have with you.

That document is the Residence Card (在留カード).

When you first arrive in Japan, you may find yourself wondering:

“Is a Residence Card the same as a visa?”

“When exactly will I need to use it?”

“Do I have to carry it with me at all times?”

In fact, the Residence Card is an essential form of ID used in nearly every administrative procedure and contract in daily life in Japan.

It would be no exaggeration to say that foreign nationals living in Japan use their Residence Card far more often than their passport.

This article takes a closer look at exactly where and when the Residence Card comes into play.

What Is the Residence Card?

The Residence Card is an official ID issued to foreign nationals who stay in Japan for more than three months.

The card contains the following information:

  • Full name
  • Nationality
  • Date of birth
  • Status of residence
  • Period of stay
  • Residence Card number
  • Whether employment is permitted

In short, it is your official proof that:

“This person is legally residing in Japan.”

Your Primary Form of ID in Japan

Just as Japanese nationals rely on their My Number card or driver’s license, the Residence Card serves as the most fundamental form of identification for foreign nationals.

※ My Number card: Japan’s national identification card (equivalent to a resident registration card)

In most situations in Japan where you need to verify your identity, you will be asked to present your Residence Card.

Signing a Rental Contract

When renting an apartment or condominium in Japan, landlords and real estate agencies will almost always ask for your Residence Card.

They need to confirm:

  • That you are residing in Japan legally
  • Your remaining period of stay
  • Your personal identity

If your period of stay is about to expire, you may also be asked to provide additional documents.

Opening a Bank Account

A bank account is essential for receiving your salary and managing daily finances in Japan.

Major banks where you will need to submit your Residence Card include:

  • Japan Post Bank
  • Mizuho Bank
  • MUFG Bank
  • SMBC (Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation)

Your Residence Card is the key document for verifying your identity and status of residence as a foreign national.

Signing Up for a Mobile Phone Plan

A Residence Card is also required when signing up for a mobile phone plan in Japan.

Major carriers such as:

  • NTT Docomo
  • au
  • SoftBank
  • Rakuten Mobile

all use the Residence Card to verify your identity.

It is essentially mandatory when purchasing a handset on an installment plan.

Starting a New Job

When joining a Japanese company, you will also be required to submit your Residence Card.

Employers need to verify:

  • Whether you are permitted to work
  • Your type of status of residence
  • Your period of stay

For those on a work visa, this is also the process by which the company confirms it can legally employ you.

Municipal Office Procedures

One of the first things you need to do after arriving in Japan is register your address at your local city or ward office.

Your Residence Card is required for this.

It is also needed for:

  • Changing your registered address
  • Enrolling in National Health Insurance
  • Registering for the National Pension
  • Obtaining various official certificates

The step-by-step process for updating your Residence Card address after moving is covered in Complete Guide to Residence Card Address Change Procedure in Japan.

Applying for a My Number Card

A Residence Card is required when applying for a My Number card in Japan.

It is used to confirm:

  • Your identity
  • Your status of residence

Visiting a Hospital

While you may sometimes only need your health insurance card at a medical facility, you may also be asked to show your Residence Card depending on the situation.

This is particularly common at hospitals you are visiting for the first time, where staff may request it to verify your identity.

Driver’s License Procedures

If you are converting a foreign driver’s license to a Japanese one, your Residence Card is required.

The driver’s license center will verify:

  • Your proof of residence
  • Your status of residence

Visa Renewal and Change of Status

The Residence Card is also used for visa-related procedures.

Common examples include:

  • Renewing your period of stay
  • Changing your status of residence
  • Applying for permanent residency

It is the most important document for proving your current residence status.

Re-entry at the Airport

Your Residence Card is also used when returning to Japan after traveling abroad.

When departing Japan using the:

  • Special Re-entry Permission

procedure, a Residence Card is required.

You will also be asked to present it upon re-entering Japan.

Applying for a Credit Card

When applying for a credit card in Japan, many issuers will ask for your Residence Card.

Card companies need to check:

  • Your period of stay
  • Your personal information

For foreign nationals in particular, the Residence Card becomes an important piece of information in the review process.

Signing Up for Online Services

In recent years, identity verification has become increasingly common when signing up for online services.

For example:

  • Opening a brokerage account
  • Signing up for an online bank
  • Various financial services

may use your Residence Card as part of their identity verification process.

You Must Carry Your Residence Card at All Times

This is something many foreign nationals are unaware of.

In Japan, mid-to-long-term residents are legally required to carry their Residence Card at all times.

This means you are expected to have it with you even when simply going out.

You must be able to present it if a police officer or immigration official requests to see it. Penalties for violating the obligation to carry it at all times are outlined in Penalties for Not Carrying Your Residence Card in Japan.

Can I Just Carry a Copy or Photo of My Residence Card?

No.

A photo or photocopy of your Residence Card cannot substitute for the original.

By law, you must carry the original card.

Always bring your actual Residence Card when you go out.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Residence Card used more often than a passport in Japan?

Yes.

For long-term residents, the Residence Card is used in the vast majority of situations.

Is a Residence Card required to open a bank account?

In most cases, yes.

It is used as the primary document for verifying a foreign national’s identity.

What should I do if I lose my Residence Card?

You will need to report the loss to the police and then go through the reissuance process at the Immigration Services Agency.

Do I really have to carry my Residence Card all the time?

Yes.

In Japan, you are legally required to carry it at all times.

Wrapping Up

The Residence Card is the most important form of ID for foreign nationals living in Japan.

It is required for virtually every aspect of daily life — renting an apartment, opening a bank account, signing up for a mobile plan, starting a job, visiting a hospital, and renewing your visa, among many others.

For foreign nationals on a long-term stay, the Residence Card will be used far more frequently than a passport.

Since Japanese law also imposes a legal obligation to carry it at all times, it is important to keep it safe and have it readily available whenever needed.

Now that you are starting life in Japan, remember that your Residence Card is not just a card — it is the most important document proving your identity and legal status in Japan.
